<p class="nl-eyebrow">Why Northlight</p>

## The freedom to make music

Recording a song used to need a studio. Now it needs a computer and the right software,
and the right software usually comes with a price, a subscription, or an account that can
be closed. Whether you get to make music shouldn't depend on any of that.

Northlight is a complete studio that costs nothing and belongs to the people who use it.

## Free, not cut down

Northlight is meant for finished records. You can record a band at low latency, comp
the best takes, mix the song and export the master without leaving it.

<div class="nl-feature nl-feature--stacked">
	<div>
		### Vocal pitch editing

		Northlight finds every note in a sung part. Move a note, split or join notes, or
		correct the whole take towards the key, and quieten the breaths between phrases.
		A correction moves a note's centre but keeps the singer's vibrato and the slides
		between notes, so it still sounds like them. You can change or undo any edit
		later.
	</div>
	<Screenshot
		src={pitch}
		alt="The pitch editor: a vocal take's notes drawn as blocks on a piano roll, with the sung pitch traced through each one and two notes labelled with how far off the key they are in cents."
		caption="A vocal take in the pitch editor, corrected towards A major."
	/>
</div>

<div class="nl-feature">
	<div>
		### Instruments to start with

		You don't need to buy anything to start writing. Northlight comes with Pulsar, a
		drum synth; Aurora, for basses and keys; Nebula, for slow, drifting pads; Starbit,
		a chiptune synth; and a sampler for your own sounds. When you want more, it runs
		your VST3 and CLAP plugins too.
	</div>
	<Screenshot
		src={pulsar}

<div class="nl-feature nl-feature--flipped">

		with loudness normalisation.
	</div>

		into any order, or start from a preset for vocals, guitars, drums or the mix bus.

## Effects with a character of their own

Alongside the everyday EQs, compressors, reverbs and delays, Northlight has seven effects
built to give a sound its character. Each has its own face, and each shows you what it's
doing to the sound as it plays.

<div class="nl-effects">
	<Effect
		name="Glue Compressor"
		kind="Bus compressor"
		use="Pulls a mix, or a group of drums or vocals, together into one performance."
		src={glue}
		alt="The Glue Compressor: a ribbon of the last three seconds of the mix going in and coming out, a gain reduction arc, and stepped Ratio, Attack and Release selectors."
	>
		A console-style bus compressor, with stepped ratio, attack and release and an Auto
		release that recovers quickly from a hit and slowly from held compression. A sidechain
		high-pass stops the low end pumping it, and Mix blends in parallel compression. Instead
		of a transfer curve, it draws the gap between the music's body and its peaks, so you can
		see how much punch you are keeping.
	</Effect>
	<Effect
		name="Orbit"
		kind="Tape machine"
		use="Adds a tape machine's warmth and soft compression to a bus or the master."
		src={orbit}
		alt="Orbit, a studio reel-to-reel tape machine: two metal reels, a tape time counter, VU meters and controls for speed, EQ, level and tape type."
	>
		The tape itself is modelled, so loud passages compress softly and the highs saturate first.
		Choose the tape speed, the EQ standard, how hard to hit the tape and which tape to use,
		then set the bias, hiss and flutter. Link keeps the loudness steady while you drive the
		input harder.
	</Effect>
	<Effect
		name="Patina"
		kind="Vintage colour"
		use="Makes a sound old, from a little warmth to falling apart."
		src={patina}
		alt="Patina, a weathered steel rack unit with six stages, Heat, Chamber, Drift, Grain, Wear and Crush, each with an amber display, and a large Age knob from New to Ruined."
	>
		Six stages age a sound in different ways: Heat drives it into tube, tape, iron or fuzz;
		Chamber adds a room, spring or plate; Drift adds wow and flutter; Grain adds vinyl crackle,
		hiss or hum; Wear takes away highs and adds dropouts; Crush lowers the bits and sample
		rate. One Age knob takes the whole thing from new to ruined, and Breath lets each stage
		wander over time.
	</Effect>
	<Effect
		name="Tape Echo"
		kind="Tape delay"
		use="Gives a part warm, wobbling echoes that can swell into feedback."
		src={tapeEcho}
		alt="Tape Echo, a cream and green 1970s-style unit with a tape loop running past a record head and four playback heads, a VU meter, and large Heads, Repeat Rate, Intensity and Echo Volume knobs."
	>
		A tape loop with four playback heads, in eight combinations, and a spring reverb. Turn the
		feedback past 100% and it runs away into oscillation. Change the repeat rate and the
		echoes bend in pitch as the motor settles. Wow, flutter and age
		wear the tape.
	</Effect>
	<Effect
		name="Bloom"
		kind="Effect reverb"
		use="Turns a sound into huge, frozen, shimmering or swelling space."
		src={bloom}
		alt="Bloom: a round display of the reverb tail as rings, with a Freeze button in the middle, and controls for Decay, Size, Shimmer and Swell."
	>
		Decays up to a minute in spaces far bigger than any real room. Freeze holds the tail for
		good, Shimmer pitch-shifts it inside the reverb so it climbs as it rings, and Swell builds
		it up like a reversed reverb. Gate cuts it short and Duck tucks it under the dry sound.
	</Effect>
	<Effect
		name="Vesper"
		kind="Vocal reverb"
		use="Gives a vocal space without clouding the words."
		src={vesper}
		alt="Vesper: a dusk-coloured reverb with lights for Singing, Kept Out and Line End, a display of the tail against the voice, and controls for Decay, Clarity, Duck and Ends."
	>
		Vesper listens to the singing. It keeps consonants, sibilance and breaths out of the
		reverb, lowers the tail while the singer sings, and lets it bloom at the end of each line.
		With lookahead on, it hears what's coming and throws the last word of a line. Catch holds
		the tail as a pad under the next phrase.
	</Effect>
	<Effect
		name="Corona"
		kind="Guitar amp"
		use="Plays a guitar or bass through a classic two-twelve combo, no amp needed."
		src={corona}
		alt="Corona, a tube guitar amp with a gold control panel for preamp, reverb, tremolo and power, a woven grille cloth and a Corona badge."
	>
		Modelled stage by stage on a 1960s American combo with spring reverb and tremolo, from the
		first tube to the speaker. Turn the cabinet page for five cabinets or your own impulse
		response, and place two mics on the speaker: dynamic, ribbon or condenser, near or far.
	</Effect>
</div>

<div class="nl-feature nl-feature--stacked">
	<div>
		### Guitar pedals

		Put Corona at the end of a pedalboard. Northlight has five stompboxes voiced for guitar
		and bass, each with the few controls a pedal has: Polaris, a chromatic tuner, from a
		five-string bass's low B up; Gravity, a compressor and sustainer; Flare, a distortion
		with silicon, LED or no clipping diodes; Tide, a chorus and vibrato that keeps the low
		end solid on bass; and Comet, an analog delay whose repeats fade into a warm smear, or
		run away if you let them. Click the footswitch, or press Space, to stomp on one. None of
		them adds latency, so you can play through them live.
	</div>
